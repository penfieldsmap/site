The surgeon Wilder Penfield operated on patients who were awake, stimulated the exposed brain with an electrode and recorded what they reported. On the temporal lobe, in 40 of 520 patients, the current brought voices, music and scenes that he read as a record of the patient’s own past. This map shows where, case by case, with what later work found. Devices are now being built to read the brain and write to it. Select a name on the plate, a region, or a line in the key. Left and right arrow keys step through the records. The plate is an original schematic of one hemisphere seen from the side and is not to scale. Shading marks the region a source names, not the points it plotted. Broken outlines are structures on the inner face of the hemisphere or deep inside it, projected onto this view. The first, second and third temporal convolutions are the superior, middle and inferior temporal gyri. The dark mark inside the fissure is the anterior transverse gyrus of Heschl, where stimulation gave crude sound and no memory-like experience. The side of the brain for each finding is given in its record. The method codes in the records are explained under Methods.
