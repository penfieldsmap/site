Under stimulation

DES
Penfield’s patients were awake. Each response was dictated to a stenographer and tied to a numbered ticket on the cortex. In one 1937 case the record includes tingling in the little finger, numbness in the shoulder, a shock-like feeling in four fingers that spared the thumb, and a feeling that the thumb moved when it did not. [Penfield & Boldrey 1937] Applies to: all sensory labels
DES
In a series of 50 patients mapped from 2008 to 2016, 258 of 647 sites on the crown of the postcentral gyrus gave a sensation (40%), at a mean threshold of 2.67 mA. Tingling was almost the only sensation. One patient reported pain, one a bad taste and one burning in the hand. No patient reported a sense of movement. [Roux et al. 2018] Applies to: all sensory labels
DES
The 2018 series ordered the face, tongue excluded, from medial to lateral: eyebrows, eyes, cheeks, both lips, teeth, gums, jaws, pharynx, larynx. The order rests on few sites, one each for eyebrows, eyes, nose, face, gums and pharynx. In the paper’s own table the teeth sites lie below the gums and jaw sites. [Roux et al. 2018] Applies to: sensory figure: face, lips, teeth and pharynx

Injury, disease and surgery

CLIN
Infarcts in the territory of the middle cerebral artery, which supplies most of the lateral surface, typically cause weakness that is worse in the opposite arm and face than in the leg. Sensory loss mainly affects the face and arm. Occlusion of the main stem causes weakness of the whole side. [Lui et al., StatPearls; MSD Manual; Margetis & Sánchez-Manso, StatPearls] Applies to: arm, hand, face and mouth labels, both figures

Revisions and disputes

DES
According to the 2018 authors, Penfield found 25% of sensory responses outside the postcentral gyrus. Their own series found none at 1,025 sites stimulated outside it. [Roux et al. 2018] Applies to: all sensory labels
ANAT
Primary somatosensory cortex comprises four cytoarchitectonic areas. Area 3a lies in the fundus of the central sulcus and area 3b on its posterior bank. Area 1 lies on the crown of the postcentral gyrus and extends into the postcentral sulcus. Area 2 lies mainly on the anterior bank of the postcentral sulcus. The borders vary between brains. [Geyer et al. 1999; Grefkes et al. 2001; Roux et al. 2018] Applies to: all sensory labels
SEEG
Surface stimulation in the 2018 series reached only area 1 on the gyral crown. Depth-electrode stimulation in 20 patients (149 positive sites of 177, most in sulcal cortex) found a gross medial-to-lateral order across all of primary somatosensory cortex that was consistent with the classical homunculus. [Roux et al. 2018; Sun et al. 2021] Applies to: all sensory labels
DES
According to the 2018 authors, 13% of face and tongue sensory responses in Penfield’s work (27 of 202) were felt on both sides. Their own series recorded none. [Roux et al. 2018] Applies to: sensory figure: face and mouth
MEG
An MEG study found same-side activation of primary somatosensory cortex after touch to the upper lip in 69% of cases, to the lower lip in 85% and to the tongue in 88%. [Disbrow et al. 2003] Applies to: sensory figure: lips and tongue

The 1950 figures

HIST
The 1937 figure rested on 126 usable operation records out of 163 operations done under local anaesthesia from 1928 to 1936. Boldrey compiled 170 summary charts, one for each movement or sensation. The 1950 figures drew on about 400 craniotomies, including a second series from 1936 to 1947. [Penfield & Boldrey 1937; Penfield & Rasmussen 1950; Catani 2017] Applies to: all 1950 labels
HIST
Hortense Pauline Cantlie drew the homunculus. One combined figure appeared in the 1937 paper. For the 1950 book she drew two new figures, one sensory (Fig. 17) and one motor (Fig. 22). In the reproductions viewed, both drawings carry her monogram and the year 1948. [Penfield & Rasmussen 1950; Pogliano 2012; Stuart et al. 1951] Applies to: all 1950 labels
HIST
Penfield and Rasmussen wrote that the motor homunculus may be used as an aid to memory and called it “a cartoon of representation in which scientific accuracy is impossible” (p. 56). Of the sensory homunculus they wrote that such drawings become confusing if too much significance is attributed to shape and comparative size, and that the indication of extent under the figure corrects this (p. 214). [Penfield & Rasmussen 1950] Applies to: all 1950 labels
HIST
Every body-part label on the 1950 plates except Gen. has a block line along the cortex. The three function words on the motor figure are brackets and have no line. The legend of the sensory figure says the length of these lines shows the comparative extent of each representation more accurately than the drawing. Wright (1956) measured every line, 28 on the sensory figure and 22 on the motor figure. The legend of the motor figure does not repeat the statement. [Penfield & Rasmussen 1950; Wright 1956] Applies to: all 1950 labels
HIST
Penfield and Boldrey scaled the 1937 figure by the vertical extent of each part along the central sulcus, without statistics. A 2017 reanalysis found that the figure was not in proportion to the paper’s own measurements. Stimulus intensity, number of patients, number of stimulations per patient and overlap between parts were not taken into account. The reanalysed sensory maps overlap substantially, mostly near the central sulcus. [Penfield & Boldrey 1937; Catani 2017] Applies to: all 1950 labels
HIST
The 1937 figure was a single drawing without labels, not laid on the brain. The 1950 figures are each laid on the outline of a coronal section, with named labels and block lines. The sensory figure adds the genitals and an intra-abdominal field. The motor figure adds three function brackets. [Penfield & Boldrey 1937; Penfield & Rasmussen 1950] Applies to: all 1950 labels
